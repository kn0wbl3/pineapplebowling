A handicap gives bowlers of every skill level a fair shot at winning. Each game, your handicap is added to your actual score (your "scratch" score). Lower averages get more pins, so a newer bowler can compete with a league veteran.

## The Formula

The league handicap is **90% of 200**. That means:

<div class="box-note" markdown="1">

**Handicap = (200 − your average) × 90%**, with fractions dropped

</div>

1. Start with 200.
2. Subtract your average.
3. Multiply by 0.9.
4. Drop anything after the decimal point. Don't round up.

Your average also drops its fractions. An average of 152.5 counts as 152.

If your average is 200 or higher, your handicap is 0.

## Example

Your average is 150.

| Step | Math | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Subtract your average from 200 | 200 − 150 | 50 |
| Take 90% | 50 × 0.9 | 45 |
| **Handicap** | | **45** |

If you bowl a 160, your score for that game is 160 + 45 = **205**.

## How Handicap Affects Scoring

Every point in the league is decided by total pins: scratch + handicap. Your handicap is added to each of your 3 games, and those totals count toward your team's games and the Wood. See [Scoring](rules#scoring) for how points are awarded.

## Which Average Is Used

Your handicap depends on which average you're bowling with, and that changes early in the season:

* **Returning bowlers** use last season's final average until they've bowled 6 games.
* **New bowlers and subs with no entering average** set their average on the first night.
* **Bowlers with an entering average** use it until they've bowled 6 games.

See [Averages and Handicaps](rules#averages-and-handicaps) for the full rules and worked examples.
